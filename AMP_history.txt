Open questions requiring decisions

1. What is authoritative for session state?

The original design says the server revalidates or rejects stale versions, but current server actions are stateless. There are two viable models:

Recommended: local-first session snapshot

- Persist one schema-versioned SessionSnapshot.
- Store responses, accepted deltas/effects, extraction state, selected race, and cached profile together.
- Use one pure client-side reducer.
- Server actions validate extraction output and tag it with baseProfileVersion.
- The client accepts a result only if its current version still matches.
- This does not provide cross-device synchronization or tamper resistance.

Alternative: durable server sessions

- Add a database-backed session authority.
- Server commits each turn and rejects stale versions authoritatively.
- Supports cross-device state and stronger integrity.
- Considerably larger scope, with privacy and retention implications.

Decision needed: Is local-first state sufficient? The existing application architecture strongly favors yes.

2. Who is allowed to define structured preference effects?

Current questions and options are generated by an LLM. Structurally valid metadata from an LLM is not necessarily semantically correct.

Recommended: registered question definitions

- Server-owned definitions contain immutable option IDs and trusted effects.
- The LLM selects a questionDefinitionId and may provide presentation wording within constrained bounds.
- Submitted option IDs are looked up server-side.
- Dynamically invented questions are non-scoring or their answers go through extraction.

Alternative: validate LLM-generated effects

- Let the LLM invent preferenceEffects.
- Validate only their schema and taxonomy membership.
- Easier, but polarity or proposition semantics may disagree with visible wording.

Decision needed: Should all scoring structured questions come from a registered catalog? The architecture review recommends yes.

3. How should canonical preferences represent state?

The current interface cannot cleanly distinguish:

- resolved stance;
- explicit neutrality;
- unresolved contradiction;
- importance-only signals;
- unmapped clarification signals;
- retractions.

A discriminated union is likely needed, along with one active aggregate per canonical key.

Decision needed: Confirm a canonical key such as:

(taxonomyVersion, propositionId, voteLane)

Also decide how a both preference interacts with later party-only or electorate-only refinements. Recommended behavior:

- both supplies a shared default;
- a lane-specific position overrides it for that lane;
- only one effective position contributes per proposition and lane;
- all source responses remain in provenance.

4. What constitutes correction versus contradiction?

Free text can say:

- “Actually, I changed my mind” — correction;
- “I support it only if…” — qualification;
- “I’m conflicted” — unresolved;
- “This is no longer important” — importance change;
- “Ignore my previous answer” — retraction.

The extraction model must return constrained operations rather than arbitrary preference IDs.

Recommended operations:

- upsert;
- retract;
- markUnresolved;
- setImportance.

Operations target canonical proposition/lane keys, not generated preference IDs.

5. How should legacy sessions migrate?

The spec proposes rebuilding old response-only sessions, potentially sending historical free text to an external model on page load. The review rejects doing that silently.

Options:

1. Recommended: start a new schema-versioned session namespace and ask users to reset or explicitly migrate.
2. Deterministically migrate only structured responses; keep unsupported responses pending.
3. Explicitly ask permission before sending historical free text for extraction.
4. Leave old sessions on the legacy ranker until reset.

Decision needed: whether preserving existing local sessions is important enough to justify an explicit migration UI.

6. Privacy and retention policy

Political preferences are sensitive. The implementation needs clear rules for:

- what remains in browser storage;
- what free text/profile content is sent to OpenAI, Anthropic, or OpenRouter;
- whether compact active preferences are sent to the next-question model;
- whether prompts or profiles may appear in production logs;
- reset behavior;
- retention of server-side data, if server sessions are selected;
- disclosure to users.

Decision needed: approve a local-first/no-server-retention policy and production log redaction, or define another policy.

7. What counts as enough evidence to rank?

Without a minimum threshold, a candidate with no evidence gets a neutral score of 50 and could outrank a candidate with credible mild disagreement at 47.

The review recommends:

status: "ranked" | "insufficient-evidence"

Subjects below a configured coverage threshold:

- remain visible;
- display insufficient evidence rather than a compatibility rank;
- sort below scoreable subjects;
- cannot be shown as the winner.

Decision needed: initial minimum usable coverage and behavior when fewer than two subjects qualify. This should ultimately be calibrated, not guessed.

8. Final ranking result contract

The spec currently mixes subject compatibility confidence with lane-wide ranking confidence. These should be separate.

A likely result needs:

- lane;
- profile hash/version;
- corpus revision;
- scoring configuration version;
- lane ranking confidence;
- subject results;
- subject compatibility;
- subject evidence coverage;
- ranked/insufficient-evidence status;
- contribution details.

Decision needed: confirm this separation before wiring scoring into UI consumers.

How Q&A becomes a structured preference

A small AI extraction call receives:

1. The exact question shown to the voter.
2. Their exact answer.
3. The list of allowed political propositions.
4. Their existing preferences, so corrections and qualifications can be recognized.

For example:

Question: Should the government build more homes, even if councils must allow greater housing density?
Answer: Yes, but mainly near public transport.

The extractor could return:

{
  "proposition": "housing/allow-density-near-transit",
  "stance": 0.8,
  "importance": 0.7,
  "confidence": 0.9,
  "voteLane": "both",
  "qualifier": "Primarily near public transport"
}

The output is validated before it affects the profile:

- the proposition must exist;
- numbers must be within allowed ranges;
- the extractor cannot invent canonical IDs or alter provenance;
- uncertain or compound answers can produce multiple preferences—or be marked unresolved;
- the original question and answer remain attached as evidence of what the voter actually said.

If extraction fails or is uncertain, we keep the answer but do not silently score it.

What the proposition taxonomy is

It is a versioned list of political claims that both voter preferences and candidate evidence can reference.

Example:

housing/increase-homebuilding
housing/allow-greater-urban-density
housing/allow-density-near-transit
housing/increase-public-housing
transport/invest-in-public-transit
transport/build-more-roads
taxation/increase-taxes-to-fund-services
climate/reduce-emissions

The important part is that each proposition has a stable meaning. Candidate evidence is classified against the same list:

Voter:
  supports housing/allow-density-near-transit at +0.8

Candidate evidence:
  supports housing/allow-density-near-transit at +0.9

Result:
  strong alignment on that proposition

Without a shared taxonomy, we would be comparing arbitrary prose. Embedding similarity could tell us both texts discuss housing, but not reliably whether their positions agree.

Does this become another catalogue to maintain?

Yes, but it is much smaller and more stable than a question catalogue.

We would not curate every possible question. We would maintain only the underlying propositions that can affect ranking. The AI remains free to ask countless personalized versions of questions about them.

When an answer expresses something outside the taxonomy, the extractor returns an unmapped preference. It remains visible in the voter profile and can guide a follow-up question, but it does not affect deterministic ranking until there is a matching proposition and candidate evidence.

My recommendation is therefore:

- AI freely generates questions.
- A versioned taxonomy defines what can be scored.
- Answer-time AI extraction maps exact Q&A pairs onto that taxonomy.
- Unmapped or uncertain views are preserved but not scored.

Main downsides

1. Similar wording does not prove agreement

NLP similarity finds passages about the same subject, but not necessarily the same position.

Voter: “Public transport funding should increase.”
Candidate: “I oppose increasing public transport funding.”

These are highly similar semantically but contradictory. Your second directional check handles this—but it must receive the full source text and reliably understand negation and qualifiers.

2. Candidates with more published material get more opportunities to score

A prolific MP might have hundreds of searchable speeches. A new independent candidate might have only a short policy page.

If we add +1 for every matching statement, the prolific speaker wins through volume. We should instead score each voter claim once per candidate:

claim 1: best evidence says aligned       → +1
claim 2: best evidence says contradictory → -1
claim 3: no reliable evidence              → unknown

Multiple candidate statements can increase our confidence in that classification, but should not create repeated points.

3. Repeated material can look like independent confirmation

The same policy may appear in a manifesto, press release, speech, and syndicated article. Those should be deduplicated so repetition does not multiply influence.

4. 0 must mean unknown, not neutral

If a candidate has no relevant evidence, that does not mean they hold a neutral position.

We should track two results separately:

alignment: based only on claims where we found decisive evidence
coverage:  how many of the voter’s claims had decisive evidence

For example:

Candidate A: 8 aligned, 2 contradicted, 20 unknown
Alignment: 80%
Coverage: 33%

Candidate B: 18 aligned, 10 contradicted, 2 unknown
Alignment: 64%
Coverage: 93%

Candidate A should not automatically rank above B without clearly showing that its apparent alignment is based on much less evidence.

5. Some claims are more important than others

If all 30 claims count equally, a minor preference such as parking rules could cancel out a defining view on healthcare.

We could initially give everything equal weight, but asking the voter to identify a few especially important points would make the result more representative without requiring decimal strength estimates.

“I support denser housing near train stations, provided infrastructure is funded first.”

- support density near stations;
- support infrastructure funding;

loses the fact that the first support is conditional on the second. The extractor must preserve relationships such as conditions, trade-offs, and exceptions. If it cannot do so confidently, that claim should remain unresolved rather than being forced into +1 or -1.

Clarifications and changed opinions

If the user adds:

“Actually, school funding isn’t essential; transport capacity is my main condition.”

We update the claim to version 3:

{
  "condition": "Sufficient public-transport capacity must be funded"
}

Then:

- relationships for claim version 2 become stale;
- evidence passages can usually be reused;
- each passage is reclassified against version 3;
- retrieval runs again to find passages specifically discussing transport capacity;
- unrelated claims and their classifications remain untouched.

If the user fully changes their mind, the old claim remains for audit but becomes superseded. It no longer contributes to ranking.

Candidate-level classification

After collecting evidence relationships, we aggregate them per candidate:

Candidate A:
  2 aligned
  1 partially aligned
  0 opposed
  → aligned, with incomplete evidence on the condition

The aggregate should be deterministic rather than another holistic AI judgment. AI classifies individual evidence relationships; ordinary code combines those classifications.

A generated explanation may then say:

Candidate A broadly aligns with your preference for transit-oriented density. Two statements support denser housing near commuter hubs, but available evidence does not clearly commit to funding schools and utilities.

The quotes and sources remain available for inspection.

Critical limitations

“All related statements” is not realistically guaranteed

Vector search returns the most relevant passages, not every relevant passage. We can improve recall by:

- combining semantic and keyword search;
- querying the claim and each condition separately;
- searching within each eligible candidate/party rather than only one global top-k;
- increasing retrieval depth;
- recording retrieval configuration and corpus revision.

But we must describe the result as the relevant evidence found, not all statements ever made.

Evidence volume remains uneven

A sitting MP may have years of Hansard material; a first-time candidate may have one webpage. We must show coverage and avoid treating silence as opposition.

Repetition must be deduplicated

Ten copies of one press release should count as one underlying position. We need a source-lineage or independence key.

Old and new positions may conflict

A candidate may genuinely change their view. The aggregation needs to show temporal conflict and generally prefer newer explicit policy without deleting historical evidence.

Candidate and party positions are different

For an electorate candidate, we may show:

- their own statements;
- their party’s policy;
- where those conflict.

Party evidence should not silently be presented as a personal statement. The eventual weighting between personal and party positions remains a separate decision.

Conditions are the hardest part

+0.5 works when the core direction agrees but an important condition is absent or differs. However:

- missing discussion of a condition may mean partially aligned;
- explicit rejection of the condition may mean mixed or even opposed;
- an unrelated vague statement should be unclear, not +0.5.

The classifier therefore needs both a label and a short reason. Tests should cover these distinctions.
